One record should follow the patient — not the other way around.

Setu is a care-access platform that links sub-centres, primary health centres, rural hospitals and district hospitals into a single continuous thread — so a patient's history, referral and follow-up travel with them, even where the network doesn't.

The distance isn't only measured in kilometres.

Long travel to the nearest specialist is only the start. Diagnostics run irregularly, medical records stay on paper or in silos, referrals lose momentum between facilities, and language, connectivity and cost decide who actually gets seen. Frontline staff are stretched thin and often cut off from the patient once a referral leaves their hands — the system has been built stop by stop, not as one continuous line of care.

Eight parts, one continuous system

Setu isn't a single app bolted onto the clinic — it's the connective layer underneath teleconsultation, referrals, records and supply, built to keep working when the signal doesn't.

i

Assisted teleconsultation

A health worker at the sub-centre brings the specialist into the room over low-bandwidth video or voice, with local-language prompts guiding the exam.

ii

Appointment & queue management

Slots and walk-in queues sync across facilities so patients wait once, not four separate times across four visits.

iii

Digital triage

Structured symptom capture at first contact routes the patient to the right tier of care immediately, rather than after a wasted trip.

iv

Longitudinal patient records

One record per patient, built up across every visit and every facility, readable by whoever sees them next.

v

Referral tracking

A referral is followed through to its outcome — no patient disappears between the PHC that sent them and the hospital that should receive them.

vi

Diagnostic coordination & medicine availability

Lab bookings and stock visibility so a patient isn't sent for a test the facility can't run, or a drug the pharmacy doesn't stock.

vii

High-risk follow-up

Patients who need to be checked on — pregnancies, chronic conditions, post-discharge recovery — are flagged and tracked until closed, not forgotten.

viii

Facility dashboards

Administrators see load, gaps and outcomes across their catchment, so decisions are made on what's actually happening, not what was last reported.

One referral, followed end to end

A working example of what changes for a patient moving from a village sub-centre to specialist care.

01

An ASHA or health worker records symptoms through digital triage in the patient's own language. The system suggests whether this needs local care or a referral up the chain.

02

Where a specialist isn't on-site, assisted teleconsultation brings one in over the available connection — audio-first if that's all the signal allows.

03

If escalation is needed, the referral travels with the patient's record attached — the receiving facility isn't starting from zero.

04

Before the patient travels, the system checks the test can actually be run and the medicine is in stock at the destination.

05

If flagged high-risk, the patient is checked on after discharge — and that outcome is written back to the same longitudinal record where it started.

Built for the person holding the tablet, not just the patient in front of them

Frontline workers are the system's real interface. Setu is designed around their constraints first — patchy connectivity, mixed literacy, and multiple languages in the same clinic queue.

Designed for the field, not the demo

  • Works offline-first, syncing records when a connection becomes available
  • Multilingual interaction, including voice input for lower-literacy users
  • Emergency escalation that reaches a human when triage flags urgency
  • Interoperable with existing health records rather than replacing them
  • Built to strengthen the public health system, not sit apart from it

What changes when the record follows the patient

Fewer* repeat trips

Diagnostics and stock confirmed before a patient travels, not after they arrive.

No lost* referrals

Every referral is tracked to a documented outcome, visible to both ends.

Continuity* of care

One record, readable at every tier, instead of four incomplete ones.

*Outcome targets to be validated against pilot data across participating PHCs and rural hospitals.
